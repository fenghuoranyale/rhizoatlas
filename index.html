Expression across species

Analyze your data

Upload gene counts from your own RNA-seq. RhizoAtlas predicts the organ identity of every sample and, when the experiment includes rhizome together with root or aerial stem, places it on the atlas scale with the article's method. Your data stay in your browser.

1 · Your count table

Paste or upload raw gene counts: one gene per row, one sample per column.

Input format

Raw read counts per gene (not TPM or FPKM) for one experiment: gene IDs in the first column, one sample per further column, sample names in the first row. Gene IDs can be Arabidopsis identifiers (after mapping your genes to their best Arabidopsis hit), RefSeq gene IDs (LOC…) from the ginger, turmeric, lotus, Coptis or banana genome, or RhizoAtlas orthogroups. Include all genes, also those with zero counts. Sample names containing rhizome, root, stem or leaf are assigned to that organ automatically. Giving your species excludes it from the atlas comparisons. For many files, the same analysis runs on the command line with rhizoatlas.py (see About).

Details and method

Score a gene set

Paste a list of genes, such as a pathway, your differentially expressed genes or GWAS candidates, to test whether they are higher in rhizomes than in roots and aerial stems across species. Scores use the matched-background method of the article's trait indices.

Scores by species

Published trait-index tests and method

For each study and species, the rhizome-minus-comparator difference of every expressed orthogroup is corrected by the mean difference of orthogroups matched for expression level and leaf depletion; a gene-set score is the mean corrected difference of its orthogroups, and a species score is the median over studies. P values come from one-sided sign tests across species; the published tests also report phylogenetic regression (PGLS), which this tool does not compute.

Find candidate genes

Rank gene families by how consistently they differ between rhizome and root or aerial stem across species, with their gene IDs in five crop genomes. The default settings give the seven-family core set reported in the article.
